Electrify Without Upgrade: grow EVs and heat pumps on wires already built.
The grid is sized for its 78.5 MW peak but averages 30.8 MW, a 39% load factor. The street transformer, not the system, is what stops electrification. Flag transformers at risk from meter data, default EV homes to smart staggered charging, add 1 or 2 leased batteries only where needed, and upgrade the rest. Every battery avoids an upgrade, then cuts the peak.

WHY  Batteries earn their place by avoiding capital, not by trading capacity
A battery fleet for the peak alone does not pay. Two units at 2,610 priority homes cost $36.5M; over 15 years lease, capacity and NYSERDA return $31M (0.85 of cost, 0.56 without NYSERDA). It breaks even only at about $129 per kW a year, against about $60 today.
Placed on an overloaded transformer, the same battery beats the upgrade. About 1.7 units (about $11,500 before NYSERDA) replace a $15,000 upgrade. In the home, only 0.7% can add a Level 2 EV within 3 kVA today; one unit raises that to 57%, two to 100%.
Simple timers backfire; smart staggered charging works. If every managed EV starts at 11pm, overloads rise from 9.8% to 16% as cars switch on together on cold nights. Spreading charging from 11pm to 3am at half power fixes most overloads at almost no cost.
The local problem is a winter problem. Electric heating homes are 11% of homes but 27% of the winter peak, and thermostat events are not acceptable on cold nights; the battery fills that gap.

What each stakeholder gains. Customers electrify without waiting for an upgrade, hosts get backup power, and everyone gets lower rates as NYSEG’s Rate Decoupling Mechanism returns the value of 23% to 44% more delivered energy to customers. Avangrid needs $137M to $363M less upgrade capital while capital spending runs at 3.4 times operating cash flow, connects customers faster toward New York’s 2030 targets, and can seek a return on avoided capital. The grid gets winter and summer relief from the same batteries.
How it holds up. Strong: segments, classification, peaks, EV start times (about 20,800 sessions) and heat pump load come from 33,757 real homes. Simulated: transformers are synthetic (8 random homes, sized to today’s peak plus 15%, overloaded above 120%); costs are market estimates; NYSEG scaling assumes our sample is typical. Ask Avangrid: transformer to meter mapping, real loading limits, NYSERDA eligibility for leased batteries, avoided capacity cost per kW.
Data: hourly AMI for 33,757 NYSEG homes (at least 90% of hours, corrected 10,001 to 20,000 upload). Market inputs: upgrade $10,000 to $20,000 (Eversource); NYISO rest of state capacity about $60 per kW a year; NYSERDA $200 per kWh; New York 2030 heat pump and ZEV targets; scaled to about 890,000 NYSEG homes.
